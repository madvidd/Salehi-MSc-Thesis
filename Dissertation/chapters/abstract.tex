\chapter*{Abstract}
Motion forecasting enables an autonomous vehicle to anticipate several plausible futures for surrounding road users rather than react only to their observed positions. The task is difficult because traffic behaviour is interactive, map-constrained and intrinsically multimodal, while an operational predictor must update continuously as new observations arrive. This dissertation investigates how the accuracy of SHARP, a short-window streaming motion-forecasting architecture, changes under controlled modifications to its attention, context and temporal encoding components.

The study first establishes local SHARP reference results on Argoverse 2 and then conducts three linked experimental stages. A ten-variant, 20-epoch screening study evaluates gated memory, cross-window consistency, learned temporal pooling, uncertainty-aware context, relative-geometry attention bias, a kinematic stem, endpoint refinement, lane topology and complete temporal-attention replacement by Mamba. Under identical controls, uncertainty-aware target context and relative-geometry bias are the only variants to improve minimum average displacement error over six modes (minADE6), reaching 0.749961 and 0.749679 respectively against 0.752339 for the shortened baseline. A separate 80-epoch attention study compares standard multi-head attention, query--key normalisation (QKNorm), Talking-Heads attention and their combination. QKNorm gives the best minADE6, 0.669777, improving its local control by 0.003683 (0.55\%). Mamba placement experiments show that a residual bidirectional module within the chronological agent-history encoder is preferable to scanning pooled scene tokens, although neither tested addition surpasses the longer unmodified SHARP reproduction.

These findings motivate a final confirmatory protocol comprising an article-aligned SHARP baseline, a QKNorm--uncertainty--geometry model, and the same model with a small residual temporal-agent Mamba module. Its result fields are deliberately reserved for validated checkpoints rather than inferred from incomplete training. The dissertation contributes a reproducible ablation framework, a placement-sensitive analysis of Mamba, and evidence that modest geometric and logit-normalisation changes are more reliable than wholesale architectural replacement.
