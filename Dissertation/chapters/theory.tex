\chapter{Background and Related Work}
\label{chap:background}

\section{Motion-forecasting formulation}

Consider a traffic scene observed at discrete times $t=-T_{\mathrm{obs}}+1,\ldots,0$. For agent $i$, the observation
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{X}_i = \left\{\mathbf{x}_i^t\right\}_{t=-T_{\mathrm{obs}}+1}^{0}
\end{equation}
contains positions and derived motion features in a local coordinate frame. The scene also contains a vector map $\mathcal{M}$ and observations of neighbouring agents. The forecasting model estimates $K$ future trajectories
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{i,k} = \left\{\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{i,k}^{t}\right\}_{t=1}^{T_{\mathrm{pred}}},
    \qquad k\in\{1,\ldots,K\},
\end{equation}
with mode probabilities $p_{i,k}$ satisfying $\sum_k p_{i,k}=1$. A mode is one distinct future hypothesis. For Argoverse 2, the experiments use $K=6$ modes and forecast six seconds from two seconds of available history, while SHARP internally divides the observations into shorter streaming windows \cite{wilson2021av2,prutsch2026sharp}.

The model must resolve three coupled sources of difficulty. \emph{Aleatoric uncertainty} is uncertainty inherent in the future: more than one manoeuvre can be correct. \emph{Interaction} means that one agent's trajectory depends on others. \emph{Map conditioning} constrains motion through lane direction, boundaries and topology. A deterministic mean trajectory can lie between two valid modes and correspond to neither one. Multimodal forecasting avoids this by predicting a small distribution of trajectories, but evaluation then has to consider both geometric error and confidence.

\section{Evaluation metrics}

Let $\mathbf{Y}_i$ be the ground-truth future and let $\lVert\cdot\rVert_2$ denote Euclidean distance. The minimum average displacement error over $K$ modes is
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{minADE}_K =
    \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}
    \min_{k}
    \left(
    \frac{1}{T_{\mathrm{pred}}}
    \sum_{t=1}^{T_{\mathrm{pred}}}
    \left\lVert \hat{\mathbf{y}}_{i,k}^{t}-\mathbf{y}_{i}^{t}\right\rVert_2
    \right).
    \label{eq:minade}
\end{equation}
It measures the average geometric error of the best available mode. The minimum final displacement error is
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{minFDE}_K =
    \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}
    \min_k
    \left\lVert
    \hat{\mathbf{y}}_{i,k}^{T_{\mathrm{pred}}}-
    \mathbf{y}_{i}^{T_{\mathrm{pred}}}
    \right\rVert_2.
    \label{eq:minfde}
\end{equation}
The miss rate (MR) is the proportion of samples for which every mode's endpoint error exceeds the benchmark threshold $\tau$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{MR}_K = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}
    \mathbb{I}\left[
    \min_k \left\lVert
    \hat{\mathbf{y}}_{i,k}^{T_{\mathrm{pred}}}-
    \mathbf{y}_{i}^{T_{\mathrm{pred}}}
    \right\rVert_2 > \tau
    \right].
\end{equation}
For the Argoverse 2 motion-forecasting benchmark, $\tau=2$ metres \cite{wilson2021av2}. The Brier-adjusted endpoint score adds a confidence penalty:
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{b\mbox{-}minFDE}_K = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}
    \min_k \left[
    \left\lVert \hat{\mathbf{y}}_{i,k}^{T_{\mathrm{pred}}}-
    \mathbf{y}_{i}^{T_{\mathrm{pred}}}\right\rVert_2
    + (1-p_{i,k})^2
    \right].
\end{equation}
All metrics in this dissertation are lower-is-better. Minimum displacement metrics are \emph{oracle} metrics: they select the best trajectory after observing the ground truth. They therefore measure whether a useful hypothesis is present, not whether the model assigned it the highest confidence. Reporting the Brier-adjusted value and MR alongside minADE and minFDE gives a less one-dimensional view.

Two reporting distinctions are essential. First, validation and test splits are not interchangeable. Second, the best minADE6 checkpoint need not share the other metric values shown at the final epoch. Where a checkpoint filename supplies only minADE6, this dissertation states that limitation rather than combining unrelated values silently.

\section{Datasets}

\subsection{Argoverse 2}

Argoverse 2 (AV2) contains motion-forecasting scenarios with tracked road users and detailed vector maps \cite{wilson2021av2}. It is the primary dataset because it provides enough scale and interaction diversity to test multi-agent context, and because the SHARP article reports both validation and challenge-test results on it. The local pipeline uses SHARP's processed single-agent train and validation scenarios. Inputs include agent positions, velocities, semantic types and lane polylines. The final architecture embeds these heterogeneous values into a shared 128-dimensional representation.

The evaluation crop and sampling follow the retained SHARP setup. For AV2, each SHARP streaming sub-window contains ten observations, representing one second, and the full local sample provides two such windows. The future contains 60 points over six seconds. Map and agent tokens outside the region of interest are excluded before scene attention. No augmentation or cross-dataset pre-training is used in the controlled studies.

\subsection{Argoverse 1 and nuScenes}

Argoverse 1 introduced a large urban forecasting benchmark with vector maps \cite{chang2019argoverse}. nuScenes provides multimodal autonomous-driving data across multiple sensors and locations \cite{caesar2020nuscenes}. Secondary Lab 2 SHARP evaluations on these datasets are included to document reproduction behaviour, not to combine incomparable leaderboard settings. In particular, the retained local nuScenes vector uses six modes while the article challenge row uses five; the split and mode count are stated with every comparison.

\section{From recurrent interaction models to vectorised scenes}

Early learned trajectory predictors relied on recurrent neural networks. Social LSTM assigned a recurrent state to each person and pooled neighbouring hidden states to model interaction \cite{alahi2016sociallstm}. Social GAN added adversarial training and a variety loss to encourage multiple socially plausible trajectories \cite{gupta2018socialgan}. Trajectron++ combined a graph-structured recurrent model, heterogeneous agent types, map context and optional dynamic constraints \cite{salzmann2020trajectron}. These models established two principles that remain relevant: an agent's history should be encoded as an ordered sequence, and interaction cannot be reduced to independent per-agent extrapolation.

Rasterising a scene into a bird's-eye-view image makes convolutional processing convenient but can discard the exact structure of map polylines and add substantial computation. VectorNet instead represents trajectories and map elements as vectors, applies local subgraph encoding and then models global interaction \cite{gao2020vectornet}. LaneGCN builds explicit lane graphs and passes messages through actor-to-lane, lane-to-lane, lane-to-actor and actor-to-actor relations \cite{liang2020lanegcn}. These approaches motivated the vector-token representation used by many later forecasting models, including SHARP.

HiVT decomposes vectorised forecasting into local context extraction and global interaction, while exploiting translation and rotation invariance \cite{zhou2022hivt}. QCNet develops a query-centric coordinate system that makes scene representations reusable as the observation window advances \cite{zhou2023qcnet}. These designs demonstrate that accuracy and online efficiency are affected not only by the decoder but by the reference frame, token hierarchy and re-use of scene computation.

\section{Transformer attention}

For a token matrix $\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}$, scaled dot-product attention first forms queries, keys and values:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{Q}=\mathbf{X}\mathbf{W}_Q,\quad
    \mathbf{K}=\mathbf{X}\mathbf{W}_K,\quad
    \mathbf{V}=\mathbf{X}\mathbf{W}_V.
\end{equation}
The output is
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{Attention}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{K},\mathbf{V}) =
    \mathrm{softmax}\left(\frac{\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^{\top}}{\sqrt{d_h}}+\mathbf{B}\right)\mathbf{V},
    \label{eq:attention}
\end{equation}
where $d_h$ is the per-head dimension and $\mathbf{B}$ contains masks or learned biases. Multi-head attention repeats this calculation in separate learned subspaces and concatenates the results \cite{vaswani2017attention}. A head can specialise in a particular interaction pattern, while the feed-forward and residual layers refine the aggregated representation.

Attention is well suited to motion forecasting because any relevant agent or lane token can interact directly with another. Its weakness is that the dot-product logit depends on both direction and magnitude. Large query or key norms can make the softmax distribution sharply concentrated before that concentration has acquired a useful semantic meaning.

\subsection{Query--key normalisation}

QKNorm normalises each query and key vector and learns a scale $g_h$ for head $h$ \cite{henry2020qknorm}:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\mathbf{q}} = \frac{\mathbf{q}}{\max(\lVert\mathbf{q}\rVert_2,\epsilon)},
    \qquad
    \hat{\mathbf{k}} = \frac{\mathbf{k}}{\max(\lVert\mathbf{k}\rVert_2,\epsilon)},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \ell_h(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{k}) = g_h\hat{\mathbf{q}}^{\top}\hat{\mathbf{k}}.
    \label{eq:qknorm}
\end{equation}
The logit now depends on angular similarity and a trainable head-level temperature. The hypothesis in this project is not that QKNorm adds scene knowledge, but that it improves optimisation of the attention already responsible for temporal and spatial interaction.

\subsection{Talking-Heads attention}

Standard heads are independent until their outputs are concatenated. Talking-Heads attention introduces learned linear mixing across heads before and after softmax \cite{shazeer2020talkingheads}. If $\mathbf{L}$ is the stack of head logits, a simplified representation is
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{A} = \mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{post}}
    \;\mathrm{softmax}\left(\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{pre}}\mathbf{L}\right),
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{pre}}$ and $\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{post}}$ mix the head dimension. This allows complementary heads to exchange evidence. The additional flexibility can also be redundant in a compact model, so it requires a controlled experiment rather than an assumption of improvement.

\subsection{Relative-geometry bias}

Transformers require positional information because attention itself is permutation invariant. Relative-position attention shows that pairwise relation features can be incorporated directly into the score \cite{shaw2018relative}. Motion interaction is explicitly geometric, so the Lab 2 bias uses the normalised displacement $\Delta x,\Delta y$, distance $r$, and periodic heading difference $\cos\Delta\theta,\sin\Delta\theta$. A small multilayer perceptron maps this relation vector to one additive bias per attention head:
\begin{equation}
    b_{ij}^{h}=f_h\left(
    \frac{\Delta x_{ij}}{30},
    \frac{\Delta y_{ij}}{30},
    \frac{\lVert\Delta\mathbf{x}_{ij}\rVert_2}{30},
    \cos\Delta\theta_{ij},
    \sin\Delta\theta_{ij}
    \right).
    \label{eq:geometrybias}
\end{equation}
The final layer is zero-initialised, so the modified model begins with the baseline attention scores and learns geometry only when it lowers the training objective.

\section{State-space models and Mamba}

A continuous linear state-space model represents a latent state $\mathbf{h}(t)$ driven by input $\mathbf{x}(t)$:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d\mathbf{h}(t)}{dt}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{h}(t)+\mathbf{B}\mathbf{x}(t),
    \qquad
    \mathbf{y}(t)=\mathbf{C}\mathbf{h}(t)+\mathbf{D}\mathbf{x}(t).
\end{equation}
After discretisation, the recurrent form becomes
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{h}_{t}=\bar{\mathbf{A}}_{t}\mathbf{h}_{t-1}+\bar{\mathbf{B}}_{t}\mathbf{x}_{t},
    \qquad
    \mathbf{y}_{t}=\mathbf{C}_{t}\mathbf{h}_{t}+\mathbf{D}\mathbf{x}_{t}.
    \label{eq:ssm}
\end{equation}
Mamba makes selected SSM parameters input-dependent, so the recurrence can retain useful information and suppress irrelevant information on the basis of the current token \cite{gu2024mamba}. A hardware-aware selective scan evaluates the recurrence efficiently. Unlike pairwise attention, the computational cost scales linearly with sequence length.

Linear complexity alone is not sufficient reason to use Mamba in SHARP. The observed agent sequence has only ten points per short window, so quadratic attention is not a bottleneck there. The stronger motivation is inductive bias: recurrence matches chronological order and can summarise motion state. Conversely, applying Mamba to an arbitrary list of pooled scene tokens gives the state transition no stable temporal meaning. The project therefore distinguishes three cases: scene-token addition, residual temporal-agent addition and complete temporal-attention replacement.

DeMo is important supporting evidence because it uses unidirectional Mamba for observed histories and bidirectional Mamba in future-state and coupling modules, together with attention \cite{zhang2024demo}. It demonstrates that attention and selective state-space processing can be complementary in motion forecasting. It does not establish that SHARP benefits from the same configuration, because the streaming mechanism, decoder and sequence lengths differ.

\section{Streaming motion forecasting}

Snapshot prediction discards the relationship between consecutive invocations. RealMotion instead accumulates scene context and relays previous trajectories as driving continues \cite{song2024realmotion}. SEAM uses endpoint-aware modelling to retain useful context efficiently \cite{prutsch2026seam}. SHARP processes non-overlapping short windows and associates latent state with agent identity, allowing an agent observed briefly in the current window to benefit from earlier matched observations \cite{prutsch2026sharp}.

SHARP's innovation has three consequences for this dissertation. First, a valid ablation must leave the identity-based context stream intact unless the stream itself is the tested variable. Second, evaluation should include short and heterogeneous observation histories rather than only one fixed context. Third, a modification that improves a snapshot metric but destroys incremental state reuse is not an uncomplicated improvement. For this reason, the experiments change local operators while preserving windowing, memory transfer, target-centric context, trajectory relay and dual training.

\section{Research gap}

The literature supplies strong individual mechanisms but limited evidence about their interaction inside a compact streaming model. QKNorm has a clear optimisation rationale, relative geometry has a clear scene prior, and Mamba has a clear sequential rationale. What is missing is a controlled placement study that holds SHARP's streaming system and optimisation fixed. The experimental programme therefore progresses from broad independent screening, through a full-length attention ablation, to a narrow confirmatory combination. This staged design reduces the risk of attributing an improvement to a component that merely benefited from a different schedule, batch or checkpoint rule.
